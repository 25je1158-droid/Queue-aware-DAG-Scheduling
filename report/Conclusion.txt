## Conclusion

This project develops a trace-driven simulation of queue-aware online DAG task scheduling and offloading in a vehicular edge-computing environment. It combines SUMO mobility traces, DAG precedence constraints, distance-dependent upload-delay estimation, heterogeneous MEC servers, dynamic server queues, mobility-feasibility screening and critical-path-weighted CPU allocation.

The proposed online heuristic estimates upload and server workload delays for feasible MEC candidates and selects the candidate with the lowest predicted completion delay. During execution, each MEC server admits uploaded tasks to a waiting queue, starts tasks when a parallel execution slot is available, and divides its CPU capacity among running tasks according to their critical-path ranks. The implementation records task-, application- and server-level performance metrics and compares the proposed server-selection policy with Random, Nearest and Highest-CPU baselines.

The experiments show that the queue-aware policy completes all applications in the default 20-vehicle run and achieves a lower mean application completion time and queue waiting time than the Highest-CPU baseline in that run. The improvement is modest, so it should be interpreted as evidence for the configured simulation rather than as a universal or statistically conclusive result. Repeated experiments across three task seeds and three selected vehicle counts provide additional sensitivity evidence.

## Limitations and future work

The current framework uses an approximate queue-workload predictor, a simplified distance-dependent wireless model, and a conservative contact-time estimate based on speed and distance margin without vehicle heading. SUMO mobility is replayed from a CSV trace rather than coupled live through TraCI. The proposed scheduler is a greedy online heuristic, not an exact global optimizer. The current experiments reuse one mobility trace and vary vehicle subsets and task-generation seeds; they do not yet compare multiple independently generated traffic-density scenarios.

Future work can add heading-aware trajectory prediction, live SUMO-TraCI co-simulation, calibrated wireless-channel models, explicit result-transmission delay, additional traffic scenarios, energy modelling, local vehicle execution, and comparison with optimization-based or learning-based schedulers.
